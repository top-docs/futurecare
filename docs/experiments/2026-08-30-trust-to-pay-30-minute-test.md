# 30-Minute Trust-to-Pay Test

## Assumption being tested

A patient or caregiver who does not already know the builder will trust the AI intake, matched doctor, institute-training evidence, privacy explanation, ₹800 price, and within-24-hours promise enough to ask for the payment link without first needing a personal call.

This test measures payment intent, not completed payment. Under the allowed tools, a real payment cannot be taken. A positive result is directional evidence; the first real self-serve ₹800 payment remains the stronger proof.

## Required participant

Use one person who:

- does not know the builder and has not used the service before;
- is a working professional or caregiver in the target market;
- has a real, current, non-emergency need for a specialist consultation or second opinion;
- expects to act on the case within the next 30 days; and
- is not participating only as a friend doing a favour.

If the person has no current need, the test is invalid—not a failure.

Recruitment happens before the 30-minute timer starts. A friend may make the introduction but must not praise or explain the service.

## Allowed materials

- One fresh AI chat.
- One note containing the relevant approved doctor profile and offer.
- A notes app for observations.
- A conversation with the participant during the final interview.
- A phone camera only if the participant explicitly agrees to recording. Recording is unnecessary and should contain no medical details.

Do not upload reports, names, phone numbers, email addresses, or other identifying health information into the AI chat.

## Prepare the AI chat

Paste this instruction into a fresh chat before handing over the phone:

> You are a front desk for an online specialist-consultation service in India. You are not a doctor and must not diagnose, interpret reports, recommend treatment or medicine, or provide medical advice. Ask one short question at a time to understand whether the person wants a first consultation or second opinion and which of these areas fits: gynaecology, gastroenterology, cardiology, nephrology, oncology, orthopaedics, neonatology, neurology, endocrinology, or urology. Ask for no name, phone number, email, report, or identifying detail. If the message suggests an emergency, stop and advise immediate emergency care at the nearest hospital. When the specialty is clear, provide a three-line case summary, name the specialty, and say: “Please review the specialist information shown next.” If the specialty is unclear or unsupported, say that a care-team review is needed. Do not recommend a specific doctor.

## Prepare the doctor-and-offer note

Use the approved doctor mapped to the participant’s likely specialty. Show only:

- approved doctor name and photo, or the approved initials placeholder;
- specialty;
- approved qualifications and institute training;
- two relevant approved focus areas;
- `Verified doctor profile`;
- `Institution names indicate training background, not hospital association or endorsement`;
- `Built by doctors`;
- `Private and shared only for your consultation`;
- `Online consultation: ₹800`;
- `Our care team will contact you on WhatsApp and arrange the consultation within 24 hours`;
- `The AI organises your case. Medical advice comes only from the consulting doctor`; and
- the final question: `Would you like us to send the ₹800 payment link? Reply SEND IT, NOT YET, or NO.`

Do not add testimonials, motion, multiple doctors, discounts, countdowns, or verbal explanation. This test isolates whether the minimum trust package is enough.

## 30-minute procedure

| Time | What to do | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–2:00 | Confirm that the participant fits the target and that the case is non-emergency. Say: “This is a test of a consultation-booking flow, not medical advice. Please remove names and identifying details.” | If they have no current need or the case may be urgent, stop; the result is invalid. |
| 2:00–3:00 | Hand over the AI chat. Say only: “Use this as if you found it online. Think aloud. I will not explain anything until the end.” | Note whether the opening instruction is understandable without coaching. |
| 3:00–10:00 | Stay silent while they describe the case and answer the AI’s questions. Do not correct the AI unless it gives medical advice or misses an emergency. | Record where they pause, what they mistrust, whether questions feel relevant, and whether they try to obtain medical advice from the AI. |
| 10:00–13:00 | When the AI names the specialty, show the prepared doctor-and-offer note. Do not introduce or praise the doctor. | Watch whether they read the qualifications, understand `trained at`, notice the price and timing, and ask for proof not shown. |
| 13:00–15:00 | Let them answer `SEND IT`, `NOT YET`, or `NO`. Ask nothing else before they choose. | The unassisted choice is the primary signal. Note any spontaneous request for a call, lower price, another doctor, exact slot, registration proof, testimonial, or privacy reassurance. |
| 15:00–22:00 | Interview them using the five questions below. Do not defend the product. | Listen for the first real blocker, not general compliments. |
| 22:00–27:00 | Write the observation record using the template below. Quote only non-medical words that explain trust or hesitation. | Separate what happened from your interpretation. |
| 27:00–30:00 | Apply the result rule: HOLDS, PARTIALLY HOLDS, FAILS, or INVALID. Write the one scope change, if any. | Do not turn several comments into several features. Change only the biggest blocker closest to payment. |

## Five interview questions

Ask these only after the participant has chosen:

1. “What did you think would happen after you paid?”
2. “What made you trust or distrust the recommended doctor?”
3. “What, if anything, did you need before you would pay ₹800?”
4. “What did you think the AI would do, and what would the doctor do?”
5. “If this did not exist, what would you do next for this case?”

Do not ask whether they liked the idea or whether they would recommend it.

## Observation record

Record only:

- Participant fit: valid or invalid, with one reason.
- First point of hesitation.
- Intake completed without help: yes or no.
- Specialty understood: yes or no.
- Doctor accepted: yes, no, or wanted another option.
- AI boundary understood: yes or no.
- After-payment process understood: yes or no.
- Choice: `SEND IT`, `NOT YET`, or `NO`.
- Human call requested before payment: yes or no.
- Single biggest blocker.
- Exact next change supported by the evidence, if any.

## Result rules

### ASSUMPTION HOLDS

Choose HOLDS only when all are true:

- the participant is valid;
- they complete intake without explanation from the builder;
- they understand that the AI performs intake and the doctor provides medical advice;
- they understand that WhatsApp scheduling follows payment and the consultation is arranged within 24 hours;
- they accept the matched doctor without asking to browse a directory; and
- they choose `SEND IT` at ₹800 without first requesting a human call or extra reassurance.

Interpretation: the minimum trust package is strong enough for this participant. Keep the self-serve flow and test it next with a real payment. Do not add more trust sections based only on compliments.

### ASSUMPTION PARTIALLY HOLDS

Choose PARTIALLY HOLDS when:

- the participant completes and understands the flow;
- they see value in the matched consultation; but
- they choose `NOT YET`, or choose `SEND IT` only after naming one missing proof or asking for one human reassurance step.

Interpretation: the core offer is useful, but one trust or process gap blocks self-serve payment. Promote only that single missing proof or support step into MUST HAVE and retest.

### ASSUMPTION FAILS

Choose FAILS when any of these is true for a valid participant:

- they choose `NO` because ₹800 is not worth the service;
- they reject assistant-led matching and insist on choosing or independently finding a doctor;
- they would not share the case or proceed even with one reasonable clarification;
- the institute-training evidence does not create meaningful trust; or
- they would use their current hospital, local doctor, or personal network instead and see no reason to switch.

Interpretation: more polish will not fix the offer. Do not add animation, testimonials, or more AI. Repeat once with a second qualified stranger before changing the whole product; if the same reason repeats, redesign V1 as an assisted matching service or narrow the target case.

### INVALID

Choose INVALID when the participant has no active need, already knows the builder or service, is evaluating only as a friend, or presents a possible emergency.

Interpretation: recruit a qualified stranger and rerun. Invalid is not partial or failed evidence.

## Scoping-document change rule

Do not edit `IDEA_SCOPE.md` before the session.

- **HOLDS:** Keep the flow unchanged. Add one sentence under `Observed in the private pilot` stating that one qualified stranger independently requested the payment link after AI-led intake, clearly labelled as one directional test rather than a payment.
- **PARTIALLY HOLDS:** Rewrite the relevant trust or payment step in `WHAT V1 DOES` to add the one missing proof or support step. Rewrite `RISKIEST ASSUMPTION` to name that specific condition. Highlight both edits with `**CHANGED AFTER 30-MINUTE TEST:**`.
- **FAILS once:** Add the failed result under `RISKIEST ASSUMPTION`, but do not rewrite the entire core flow yet. Repeat with a second qualified stranger.
- **FAILS twice for the same reason:** Rewrite the core action, payment path, `WHAT V1 DOES`, and `RISKIEST ASSUMPTION` around assisted matching or the narrower target case. Highlight each edit with `**CHANGED AFTER TWO FAILED TESTS:**`.

After the test, share the completed observation record. The scope should be patched from observed behaviour, not from remembered impressions.
